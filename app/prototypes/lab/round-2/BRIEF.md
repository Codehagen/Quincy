# Round 2 — narrow

Christer kept **01, 02, 05, 07, 08, 13, 17, 19** (2026-11-15). In his words: he
likes the three-beat copy (what I did / what happened / what it meant) because
it makes the product feel human, and he likes how the cards look.

## What the kept pieces share — keep all of it

- **One grey stage, one white object.** A single large `#F2F2F1` stage, and on
  it one white card or window (hairline ring + very soft shadow, generous
  padding) that sits low and is cropped by the stage edge. No splits.
- **Quincy as a human editor.** Handwritten margin notes (Caveat) with a thin
  bracket, a yellow highlighter on the one sentence that matters, blue-pen
  brackets naming the beats. The product reads like someone marked up your
  page, not like a dashboard.
- **Copy in the person's voice, in three beats.** Short first-person lines, the
  number on its own line, nothing salesy. "Quincy drafts, you send."
- **Real product surfaces, calm.** Studio, the lineup week, the brain page, the
  slot grid — simplified, skeleton bars for the rest. Mono only for times,
  file names and counts.
- **Inter 14px, hierarchy by weight and grey.** One saturated accent per piece,
  most often a confident blue on the one action (Approve), yellow for the
  highlighter.
- **Motion the person causes.** Press Approve and the post slides into the
  week; drag a riff into a slot; record and the riff card slides in; a new rule
  slides into the brain.

## Round 2 asks

Stop making pictures *of* the product and start making the product's own
surfaces in this style, since the next step is components. Four groups:

- **a (21–25) Writing:** the draft, the riff, Quincy's chat reply, the brain.
- **b (26–30) Planning:** the week, a slot, Approve into the week, today.
- **c (31–35) Capture:** recording, a pasted link, a merged PR, an imported
  call, the first-run interview.
- **d (36–40) Chrome:** the app shell and sidebar, a page header, an empty
  state, a settings row, the chat composer.
