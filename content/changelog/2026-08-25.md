## A merge is an event, and the writer was never told what happened

Twelve angles off four merged pull requests had produced no draft, and the
owner had pasted his own note about one of them instead. The hooks were about
the implementation; his post was about what he did and what came of it.

Quincy now pulls three quotes out of a merge — what you did, what happened,
what it meant — and the writing is composed with you as the subject rather
than the code. The repository's own description, homepage and topics come
along, so a post is no longer written by something that does not know what the
software is. The pull request body also stops being read as a transcript of
somebody talking, and tables, images and links survive the flattener, which is
where the measured number usually lives.

The same merge that used to produce "The first background job in the product
is not a cron" now produces "I replaced a cron-shaped voice-note workflow with
a background job that starts when somebody presses stop."
