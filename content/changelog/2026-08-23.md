## Every draft was told to copy its own last draft and avoid your real posts

Two lists were handed to the writer in the wrong order, one line of
destructuring apart. "This is how you write, match it" arrived holding
Quincy's own previous drafts, and "these already went out, do not repeat
them" arrived holding the posts you actually published. So every draft this
product has ever written imitated itself and steered around you. Nothing in
the output showed it: the writing came back fluent, on length and plausible,
just not in anybody's voice.

## The writer got a 208-character summary of the 1,686 words you wrote

A rule written to stop a stranger's post being laundered into your own was
firing on your own pull requests too, so the writer was handed a summary
instead of the description you spent the afternoon on. Told to prefer the
specific detail and given none, it wrote the general version.

## The Ask field on a riff had been drawn and never connected

A labelled input and an Ask button have sat under every riff since the page
shipped, and the entire submit handler was a line that cancelled the event.
"More like the numbers, less product-speak" now reaches a real prompt, so a
riff can be steered instead of taken as offered or thrown away.

## Approving writing meant inventing a weekly commitment for one post

Approving places a post only if its channel has a free slot, so a new account
could approve something and be told, correctly and unhelpfully, that it has
nowhere to go. Post now publishes one approved version on the spot. It is
still you sending: nothing runs on its own, and the confirmation is the second
one in the product, because there is no unsend.

## Connecting a channel now writes the rhythm it implies

Connecting is the moment you say "publish here", so it is the moment two real
slots appear — Tuesday and Thursday at 08:00, visible on the lineup and
removable in one press. Only ever on a channel with none, so reconnecting
changes nothing.

## A post that had already gone out still read as "going out today"

The lineup set that line from any scheduled row whatever its state, so a post
published at 09:12 and a post the platform refused both read as though they
were still coming. Sent now means sent, and a refusal renders in the
platform's own words.
