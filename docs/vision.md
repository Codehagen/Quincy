# What Quincy is for

Quincy is a Head of Content you employ. You give it raw material — a voice note
on a walk, a call you recorded, a pull request you merged — and it drafts in
your voice, schedules, and publishes. The chat is where you talk to it. The
pages are windows onto the same agent state.

That is the product. This document is the argument for why it should exist in
that shape rather than another one, and what we are deliberately not building.

---

## The bet

**Social media is over. We are in interest media.**

Five years ago your feed showed you what the people you followed had posted.
Today TikTok, Instagram and Facebook serve you what the algorithm thinks you are
interested in right now. Vaynerchuk has been arguing this since 2025 and put it
plainly at NRF 2026: success is no longer measured in followers, it is measured
in the relevance of each individual post, because the algorithms are built on
relevance.

The consequence is uncomfortable and it is the reason this product has a market:
**twenty years of follower-building is partly commoditised.** The merit lives in
the individual post.

Two numbers make the case better than the argument does.

The same content, posted to his 15-million-follower TikTok account and to a
brand-new account with zero followers: **300,000 views against 9.4 million.**
The new account won. The algorithm was judging the post, not the poster.

The same message, published four times with small variations: **three averaged
70,000 views, the fourth did 6.3 million.** Same idea, different first line.

If both of those are true, the scarce resource is not distribution and it is not
audience. It is **original thought with a receipt attached** — and you have
maybe two or three genuinely new things to say in a week.

That is the whole design constraint. One expensive input, many cheap
adaptations. Everything Quincy does is downstream of it.

## What the bet changes

### Adapting beats copying, and it is measurable

The 70,000-to-6.3-million gap is the argument against cross-posting. Pasting the
same text everywhere is not distribution, it is the thing platforms have learned
to suppress. A thread on X and a vertical cut on TikTok are different artefacts
with different first seconds, made from one idea.

This is why Quincy's atomiser rewrites per channel rather than fanning out one
string, and why the run view shows the hooks side by side. The comparison is the
product.

### Volume is a strategy, not a personality flaw

Seven platforms is the floor Vaynerchuk names: X, Facebook and Facebook Reels,
Instagram and Reels, LinkedIn, Pinterest, YouTube Shorts and long-form. He
claims 432 posts in a day and is aiming at over a thousand separate accounts by
year end, on the logic that each account is an interest angle the algorithm can
match against an audience.

We take the volume argument and leave the thousand-accounts tactic. See
**Where we disagree** below.

### YouTube Shorts is a search surface

Shorts titles want search terms, not the TikTok caption. YouTube is the second
largest search engine and a clip there keeps earning long after the feed has
moved on. That is a per-channel authoring rule, which is exactly the kind of
thing a Head of Content should know and you should not have to.

### Organic first, paid only on what already won

Running cold media to acquire customers is finished. Post organically, then
spend only on the pieces that beat your own baseline — because an ad does not
get reach if the creative is not relevant either. Paid amplification is a
multiplier on merit, not a substitute for it.

### Jab, jab, right hook still holds

If everything you post is an ask, people learn to duck. The jabs are free value.
This is old and it is still the reason a content calendar full of launches
performs worse than one that earns the launch.

## DVL — and why nobody does it

Vaynerchuk's February 2026 framing is **Depth, Volume, Live**, and his own point
is that almost nobody does any of the three.

**Depth.** Reply to every DM and every comment while you are under 10,000
followers. He updates Kevin Kelly's *1,000 true fans* to 10,000 — ten thousand
people paying $5 a month is $600k a year.

**Volume.** Seven platforms minimum, no ceiling.

**Live.** Thirty minutes of TikTok Live daily, because the longer you are live
the more non-followers discover you. He ties this to live shopping and told NRF
that retailers not arguing about a live social shopping strategy right now are
setting up a hard 2027–2028.

Depth is the one that matters most here, and it is the least scalable. Hold that
thought.

## The content model underneath all of it

The pyramid has not changed. One long pillar piece — a keynote, a vlog, a
podcast — gets cut into short form: clips, quotes, stills, written posts, and
distributed across every channel. The original deck shows one keynote becoming
30+ pieces across 20 platforms and 35 million views.

The multi-handle strategy is not a replacement for this. It is the distribution
layer turned up.

## Where we disagree, and what we build because of it

There is a good criticism of the interest-media story, and it is the most
useful thing in the whole body of work.

TikTok did not move from the social graph to the interest graph in order to
democratise reach. The algorithm is optimised for **screen time**, not for
creators growing. Interest media gave creators more reach and less control. The
creator is the instrument, not the beneficiary.

Translated into something actionable:

> **Multi-handle volume buys you views. It does not build ownership.**
> Every new account starts at zero relational capital.

This is the wedge, and it is what Quincy is actually for.

If views are **rented** and relationships are **owned**, then the job is not to
maximise views. The job is to convert rented attention into owned relationship
before the algorithm moves on. That is Depth — the rule with the most evidence
behind it and the one nobody operationalises, because replying to everyone does
not scale for a human.

It scales for an agent that drafts and leaves the sending to you.

So:

- **We build the volume machinery** — the atomiser, per-channel adaptation,
  multiple hooks per idea — because the arithmetic is real.
- **We do not build a thousand faceless accounts.** That is renting attention at
  scale with no equity at the end of it, and it is a treadmill that stops paying
  the day you stop running.
- **We weight Depth above Volume in the product**, even though Volume demos
  better. Drafting a reply to every comment, and knowing who keeps showing up,
  are the two things that turn a view into a person.

Owned surfaces get the same treatment. A newsletter is not a terminus, it is the
one audience nobody can re-rank away from you — which is why Substack matters
more to us than its follower count suggests, and why testing an idea as a Note
before it costs you an essay is a rhythm rather than a habit.

## What this means for each surface

| Surface | What it is for | What the bet implies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Studio** | Where you talk to Quincy | The primary interface. Every page is a window onto the same state, never a separate app |
| **Channels** | Where the writing goes out | Publishing destinations only. Grouped by nothing else, because conflating inputs, outputs and chat surfaces is how a page ends up a logo wall |
| **Sources** | Where the material comes in | The mirror of Channels. Meetings, repos, notes — raw material before it is anything |
| **Rhythm** | What Quincy does on its own | Grouped by what a rhythm does, never by platform. Platform is a filter |
| **Brain** | What Quincy knows about you | The compounding asset. Everything else is downstream of it being right |
| **Lineup** | When it goes out | Timing is the lever you still control when reach is merit-based |
| **Numbers** | What happened | Against your own baseline, never against a follower count that no longer predicts anything |

## What we are deliberately not building

**A dashboard.** Aggregates belong in Numbers. A run of the atomiser should show
you the seventeen pieces it made and where each one is now — provenance, not
metrics. Nobody shows lineage; everybody shows a calendar.

**Autoposting without approval.** Quincy publishes in your name. Every rhythm
drafts; you send. The one exception would need to be a decision made on purpose,
not one that arrives as a default.

**A thousand accounts.** See above.

**Follower charts.** If the merit is in the post, a follower count is a vanity
number with a story attached. Every number in the product is stated against your
own median, because that is the only comparison that survives the shift to
interest media.
